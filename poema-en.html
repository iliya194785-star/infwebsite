Taras Shevchenko

The Great Kobzar · Symbol of Ukraine

Taras Shevchenko was a Ukrainian poet, prose writer, thinker, painter, engraver, ethnographer, and public figure. His work laid the foundation of modern Ukrainian literature and, to a significant degree, the modern Ukrainian literary language.

Childhood and Youth

Born on March 9, 1814 in the village of Moryntsi in the Kyiv Governorate, to a family of serfs. He lost his mother early, and his father when he was eleven. From childhood he endured the harsh realities of serfdom, poverty, and humiliation.

In 1828 he was taken into the household of landowner Engelhardt as a servant boy. Noticing his artistic talent, the landowner sent him to study under a painter. In 1838, through the efforts of Karl Bryullov and Vasyl Zhukovsky, Shevchenko was purchased out of serfdom for 2,500 rubles.

The Kobzar and His Art

His first poetry collection, the Kobzar, was published in 1840 and immediately brought him wide fame. It included poems such as Kateryna, Topolia, and Haidamaky. Shevchenko became one of the most active members of the Cyril and Methodius Brotherhood.

"Fight — and you shall prevail!
God is on your side!
Truth is yours, and glory too,
And freedom, holy, wide!"

Exile and Return

In 1847 Shevchenko was arrested for his involvement in the Brotherhood. He was sentenced to exile as a common soldier at the Orsk fortress, and later to the Mangyshlak peninsula. He was forbidden to write or paint.

After ten years of exile (1857), Shevchenko returned to St. Petersburg. In his final years he worked actively on new poetry, drawings, and engravings.

Legacy

Taras Shevchenko became the symbol of the Ukrainian national revival. He is the author of over 200 literary works and more than 150 works of visual art. Thousands of streets, schools, libraries, and monuments worldwide bear his name.

"And in that great family, that free and new family,
do not forget to remember me with a kind, gentle word."
